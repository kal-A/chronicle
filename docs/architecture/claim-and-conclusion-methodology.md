# Claim and Conclusion Methodology

## Atomic Claims

Extraction produces atomic proposed Claims, not free-form conclusions. Claim types include event fact, date, location, participation, communication, actor knowledge, institutional position, causal/influence, interpretation, historiographical disagreement, and uncertainty.

## Evidence Ledger

Every Claim has a ledger containing supporting, contradicting, qualifying, and contextual EvidenceLinks; source diversity and dependence; primary/secondary balance; chronology; completeness; extraction provenance; critic decisions; review status; and unresolved gaps.

A synthesis may reference only Claims present in the structured set. The composer cannot introduce a new material statement directly into prose.

## Relationships

A proposed Relationship records source/target, type, temporal order, proposed mechanism, intermediaries, supporting/counterevidence, alternative explanations, source independence, evidence classification, uncertainty, stage provenance, and review status. Chronological adjacency is never a mechanism.

## Critic Stage

The critic asks whether evidence establishes the mechanism, whether later interpretation is being presented as contemporary fact, whether intermediaries or alternatives are missing, whether the proposal depends on one author/derivative family, and whether wording exceeds evidence. It returns accept, downgrade, dispute, reject, or abstain with structured reasons.

## Conclusion Labels

Use `established_within_corpus`, `strongly_supported`, `indirectly_supported`, `contextual`, `disputed`, `speculative`, and `insufficient_evidence`. These are corpus-relative qualitative classes, not probabilities of truth.

## Abstention

Successful output includes statements such as: evidence does not establish this; current sources conflict; one perspective dominates; the question exceeds scope; needed full text was unavailable; or the requested knowledge-state inference is unsupported.
