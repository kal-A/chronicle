# Source Assessment Methodology

## Principle

Source assessment is a structured, auditable decision, not an opaque credibility score and not a determination of historical truth.

## Rubric

For each candidate record:

- relevance to the approved scope;
- source class;
- authority and provenance;
- evidentiary usefulness;
- independence/derivation;
- perspective and likely limitations;
- temporal relationship to the event;
- completeness (full text, excerpt, OCR fragment, abstract, metadata);
- rights for storage, processing, quotation, display, and linking;
- citation traceability;
- coverage contribution.

Each dimension stores a categorical result, evidence/reason, assessor type, and provenance. Ranking may prioritize work but cannot decide truth.

## Decisions

- accepted as evidence;
- accepted as contextual background;
- accepted as competing interpretation;
- discovery-only;
- deferred: full text unavailable;
- rejected: irrelevant, duplicate, unclear provenance, unsuitable rights, or weak content quality.

Rejection/defer reasons are mandatory. A candidate can be reassessed through a new immutable assessment revision.

## Deterministic Gates

Unknown rights block acquisition/display by default. Metadata-only blocks passage extraction. Untraceable content blocks claim support. Duplicate grouping does not merge distinct editions. Model-produced assessments require prompt/model/timestamp metadata and deterministic validation of enum/reason completeness.
