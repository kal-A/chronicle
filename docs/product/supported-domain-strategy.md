# Supported Domain Strategy

## Initial Domain

Start with European diplomatic and political history from 1814 to 1914, using selected congresses and crises with accessible English-language sources, reputable digital collections, manageable geographic scope, and meaningful historiographical disagreement.

Candidate investigations include the early Concert of Europe, Troppau, Laibach, Verona, selected 1848 diplomatic responses, Crimean War diplomacy, Balkan crises, and the July Crisis.

## Why This Boundary

- It offers connected entities and institutions across multiple investigations.
- Diplomatic records provide strong tests for sent, received, report, and awareness times.
- Archives and documentary editions provide a mix of structured metadata, scans, transcripts, and rights conditions.
- The period exercises disputed interpretation without requiring immediate global or multilingual coverage.
- July Crisis material remains a useful golden fixture inside the same broad domain.

## Capability Claims

Chronicle may claim support only for request types and domains represented in its evaluation set. Unsupported periods can still be scoped or stored as sources, but generation must abstain or clearly label experimental coverage when provider, language, geography, or evaluation support is inadequate.

## Expansion Gate

A domain expansion requires:

- named archive/scholarly providers and rights assumptions;
- representative golden requests and source corpora;
- extraction, claim-linking, timeline, relationship, and map evaluations;
- known language and OCR limitations;
- a documented decision that existing schemas can represent the domain honestly.

The domain boundary is configuration and evaluation policy, never a hard-coded schema enum.
