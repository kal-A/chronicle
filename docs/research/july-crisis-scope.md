# July Crisis of 1914 — Investigation Scope

## Boundary

**Start:** 28 June 1914 — assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand and Sophie in Sarajevo.
**End:** 4 August 1914 — Britain's declaration of war on Germany, generally treated as the point the crisis has fully resolved into general European war. (Austria-Hungary's declaration on Serbia, 28 July, and the subsequent cascade of mobilizations/declarations through early August form the crisis's climax; the investigation should carry through to Britain's entry as the natural close of the "crisis" period, distinct from the war itself, which is out of scope.)

This is a deliberately bounded ~5-week window. It is well-documented, has clear multi-actor decision points across at least seven governments (Austria-Hungary, Serbia, Germany, Russia, France, Britain, and to a lesser extent Italy), has genuinely disputed historiography, and is geographically and evidentially tractable for a first investigation — while still exercising every core capability in `docs/product/core-user-experience.md` (multi-actor "known at the time" reconstruction, disputed causal interpretation, primary-source density, geographic spread across ~6 capitals, institutional/ideological pressure).

## In Scope

- Key actors: heads of state/government and foreign ministries of the seven states above; key ambassadors and military leadership where their decisions/knowledge are load-bearing (e.g., Austro-Hungarian and German military leadership's mobilization pressure).
- Key decisions: the Austro-Hungarian response to the assassination, the "blank cheque," the Austro-Hungarian ultimatum to Serbia, Serbia's reply, Austro-Hungarian declaration of war, the mobilization cascade (Russia, Germany, France, Austria-Hungary), the German invasion plan's dependency on Belgian neutrality, and Britain's decision to enter.
- Key institutional/ideological/technological pressures: alliance systems, mobilization-timetable rigidity, "war by timetable" arguments and their historiographical critics, domestic political pressures in each state, the telegraph/diplomatic-communication latency that shapes "known at the time."
- Genuinely disputed historiography: relative war-guilt/responsibility across the great powers (Fischer thesis and its critics), how avoidable the outcome was, the degree to which mobilization timetables constrained political choice ("machines took over" vs. political choice narratives).

## Explicitly Out of Scope (for this investigation)

- Military operations/campaigns after the outbreak of general war.
- The Balkan Wars of 1912–13 as a full investigation (referenced as background/context only, not built out as its own investigation in Phase 1–6).
- Long-run causes predating ~1900 (alliance formation history, colonial rivalry buildup) — referenced as background context entities, not deeply evidenced in the first investigation.
- Non-European theaters and non-European actors' internal decision-making.

## Content Deliverable for Phase 0

An initial source register (see `source-register-template.md` and `july-crisis-source-register.md`) with a first pass of ~15–25 sources across the source hierarchy, covering the key decisions above, sufficient to draft the guided-narrative outline in `phase-1-scene-outline.md`. This is a curation task, not a build task: the register may remain incomplete, but the selected prototype scene must pass its passage-level content gate before implementation presents historical claims.
